# Bible Ripple — Editorial Principles

This document describes the editorial principles currently understood from the original "אדוות התנ״ך" project.

It contains both established principles and unresolved questions.

Software must not silently resolve questions that the editors have not resolved.

---

# Established principles

## 1. Peshat rather than derash

The project focuses on plain/intra-biblical meaning rather than homiletical associations.

A possible association is not automatically a Ripple.

The editorial question is whether another biblical passage genuinely illuminates, parallels, continues or explains the current passage.

---

## 2. Main categories of ripple

The original project identifies three broad purposes:

1. thematic/content parallels;
2. completion of a topic or idea;
3. explanation of something unclear.

Additional useful subtypes may emerge from the material.

---

## 3. Contradictions and tensions may be included

A Ripple may expose passages that appear to contradict or challenge one another.

The project does not have to resolve the contradiction.

Its job may simply be to put the relevant biblical texts together.

---

## 4. Primary occurrence

When a topic or idea occurs many times, the complete collection of related passages may be attached to its first or most central occurrence.

Other occurrences may point back to that central Ripple.

"First" and "central" are not necessarily identical.

---

## 5. Full quotation

In the source project, a parallel verse is generally quoted in full rather than only quoting the words directly relevant to the Ripple.

---

## 6. Clarification

When the relevance of a quoted passage is not sufficiently clear, a very short parenthetical explanation may be added.

The explanation should remain minimal.

---

## 7. Aramaic passages

Aramaic biblical passages may include a short Hebrew translation.

---

## 8. Ordering

Related sources are generally ordered according to biblical book order, although contextual considerations may justify exceptions.

---

## 9. Number of ripples

There is no predetermined quota.

Some chapters naturally contain many ripples and others contain few.

---

## 10. The Bible may interpret itself

Juxtaposing biblical passages can sometimes suggest an interpretation without adding an external commentary.

The editorial choice of a Ripple may itself therefore carry interpretive meaning.

---

# Unresolved editorial questions

## People and names

When a person first appears, how much later information about that person should be connected?

When does useful narrative completion become an encyclopedia of every appearance of that person?

No final rule currently exists.

---

## Places

Similar uncertainty exists for places.

If a city later changes hands, is destroyed, rebuilt or mentioned repeatedly, which later events belong in the Ripple?

No final rule currently exists.

---

## General wisdom statements

Should a general statement from Proverbs be linked to a narrative character who appears to exemplify it?

Examples under consideration include:

- Noah as righteous vs general statements about a righteous person;
- Noah and wine vs general warnings against drunkenness;
- Joseph's success vs general wisdom about skill/success;
- Judah's guarantee vs wisdom statements concerning guarantees.

These examples should be treated as editorial test cases, not settled policy.

---

# Editorial history

Rejected ideas should not simply disappear.

The system should preserve:

- what was proposed;
- why it was considered;
- arguments for and against;
- the final decision.

Repeated decisions may eventually become explicit EditorialRules.
